## The Story in One Paragraph

In December 1991, Bob Kuwahara's nephew dropped a Game Boy down the stairs. 
It survived. Bob realized: this $89 toy was more durable than the $899 custom 
geological instruments his failing company was building. Eight weeks later, 
they had a prototype stereonet plotter running on Game Boy. Six months after 
that, they launched GeoCalc Pocket. Over the next 19 years, they sold 74,580 
units on Nintendo hardware, changed how structural geology is taught, saved 
at least one PhD dissertation, and proved that sometimes toys make better 
tools than tools.
