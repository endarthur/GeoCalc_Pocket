## Timeline

### 1987: Founding
- **March 1987:** Company founded as "GeoStat Instruments"
- **Founders:**
  - Dr. Margaret "Maggie" Chen (CEO, age 29)
  - Robert "Bob" Kuwahara (CTO, age 31)
  - James Okoye (VP Engineering, age 28)
- **Initial funding:** $45,000 savings + $120,000 SBA loan
- **Location:** Golden, Colorado (Maggie's garage)

### 1987-1991: The Dark Period
- **1988:** FieldLogger I released (47 units sold @ $899)
- **1989:** StrikeCalc released (312 units @ $450)
- **1990:** FieldLogger II disaster (15% return rate)
- **Late 1991:** Company near bankruptcy
  - Revenue: $147,000/year
  - Burn rate: $22,000/month
  - Runway: 4 months
  - Employees: 3 founders + 1 programmer

### 1991: Name Change
- **January 1991:** Renamed to "GeoStructure Systems"
- **Reason:** Confusion with geostatistics field
- **Decision made:** Denny's in Golden during blizzard

### December 1991: The Revelation
- **Event:** Bob's nephew Danny drops Game Boy down stairs, it keeps working
- **Bob's insight:** "Game Boy is better field hardware than anything we could build"
- **Key observation:** $89 toy survives better than $899 custom instrument

### 1992: The Pivot
- **January 2, 1992:** Bob pitches Game Boy idea to founders
- **Vote:** 2-1 to proceed (Maggie initially skeptical)
- **February-April:** Prototype development
  - Bob, James, and Mark lock themselves in office for 8 weeks
  - First successful stereonet plot: Week 4, 2 AM
- **May 1992:** AAPG conference (Calgary)
  - Maggie demos prototype informally
  - 23 pre-orders @ $250 each
- **July 1992:** VC funding secured
  - Tom Hendricks, Front Range Ventures
  - $250,000 for 25% equity
  - Stipulations: Ship by December 1993, professional packaging
- **August-October 1992:** Team expansion
  - Hired: Dr. Sarah Blackburn, Lisa Nakamura, Kevin Park
  - Team now 7 people
  - Burn rate: $21,000/month, runway 11 months

### 1993: Development Hell
- **August-December 1992:** Core engine development
- **September 1992:** The Great Tile Crisis (196 tiles for stereonet, only 256 total)
- **October 1992:** Shadow buffer insight (breakthrough)
- **November 1992:** Bob's near-breakdown (throws Game Boy at wall, it survives)
- **January-April 1993:** UI development
  - The Glove Test (usability with work gloves)
  - Sunlight visibility problem solved
- **May-August 1993:** Geology validation
- **September-November 1993:** Manufacturing crisis
  - First batch: 500 cartridges, 40% corrupted
  - Team hand-programs 127 cartridges over 3 days
  - Marathon shifts, sleeping under desks

### 1994: Launch
- **February 1994:** GeoCalc Pocket released (delayed from December 1993)
- **Price:** $299.95
- **Platform:** Game Boy (MBC1, 128KB ROM, 32KB SRAM)
- **First year sales:** 380 units ($114K revenue)

### 1995: Growing Pains
- **January 1995:** Nintendo cease-and-desist letter
  - Issue: Unauthorized use of "Game Boy" trademark in marketing
  - Resolution: Add disclaimers, clarify no endorsement
  - Nintendo never bothered them again
- **Sales:** 1,100 units ($330K revenue)

### 1996: The Breakthrough
- **June 1996:** GeoCalc DX released
  - Killer feature: Link cable to PC adapter
  - Price: $349.95 (includes adapter + software)
- **The Thesis Save:** Jennifer Wu's story (April 1996)
  - Laptop with thesis data falls in creek, destroyed
  - GeoCalc cartridge saves all 87 measurements
  - Story becomes legendary, used in marketing
- **Sales:** 2,100 units ($735K revenue)

### 1997: Legitimacy
- **August 1997:** Citation in *Tectonics* journal
  - Richard Groshong paper mentions GeoCalc DX
  - First peer-reviewed citation
  - Framed and hung in office
- **September 1997:** GeoPro GB competitor launches (fails by 1999)
- **Sales:** 3,400 units ($1.19M revenue)

### 1998: Color & Expansion
- **October 1998:** GeoCalc Palm released (first non-GB platform)
- **November 1998:** GeoCalc Color released
  - Platform: Game Boy Color
  - MBC3 with real-time clock
  - 200 measurement capacity
  - Color-coded data types
- **Combined sales:** 5,800 units

### 1999: The Printer
- **Game Boy Printer support added** (free firmware update)
  - Print stereonets at outcrop
  - Tape into field notebook
  - Sales double
- **The Mine Story** (unverified)
  - Geologist in Chile identifies unstable ground
  - Section evacuated, later collapsed
  - Story spreads (may be apocryphal)
- **Sales:** 9,000 units (peak year for GB platforms)

### 2000-2004: Maturity
- **Q4 2000:** Palm OS version overtakes Game Boy in sales
- **June 2001:** GeoCalc Advance (Game Boy Advance) released
- **March 2003:** GeoCalc Advance SP (frontlit screen, rechargeable)
  - "The ultimate field tool"
  - Sarah: "This is what we should have had all along"
- **April 2004:** Trimble Navigation acquires GeoStructure Systems
  - **Purchase price:** $23 million
  - Becomes Trimble Field Solutions division
  - Staff grows to 42 employees

### 2005-2010: The Long Tail
- **Smartphone versions** in development
- **GBA SP** continues selling to loyal professionals
- **2010:** Final Game Boy platform discontinued
  - 15+ years on Nintendo hardware
- **Antarctica unit:** Original Color from 2002, still at McMurdo Station

### 2011-2013: Nostalgia & Passion
- **May 2011:** Kevin pitches AeroGeo 3D (Nintendo 3DS)
  - Approved as research project (20% time)
  - Stereoscopic aerial photo interpretation
- **December 2012:** AeroGeo 3D released (100 units, sold out in 3 months)
  - Lost $2,100
  - Kevin: "Worth every penny. Zero regrets."
- **2010 (actual year):** 15th anniversary celebration at AGU conference
  - Michael Rodriguez moment (student whose career was changed)
  - Maggie's emotional realization

### 2013: Legacy
- **Total Nintendo platform sales:** 74,580 units
- **Lifetime revenue (all platforms):** $67 million (1994-2013)
- **Impact:**
  - Used in 340+ university geology programs
  - Cited in 180+ peer-reviewed papers
  - 50+ PhD dissertations
  - Changed how structural geology is taught

## Key Moments

### The Drop Test
**When:** December 26, 1991
**Who:** Bob Kuwahara, his nephew Danny
**What:** Danny drops Game Boy down stairs, it keeps working
**Impact:** Bob realizes Game Boy is superior field hardware

### The Glove Test
**When:** January-April 1993
**Who:** Dr. Sarah Blackburn
**What:** Test UI with Mechanix work gloves in field conditions
**Impact:** Redesigned interface for field usability

### The Thesis Save
**When:** April 1996
**Who:** Jennifer Wu, grad student at University of Arizona
**What:** Laptop falls in creek, thesis data saved on GeoCalc cartridge
**Impact:** Became legendary story, framed email in office

### Bob Throws Game Boy
**When:** November 1992, 4 AM
**Who:** Bob Kuwahara
**What:** Frustrated, throws Game Boy at wall as hard as he can. It still works.
**Impact:** Realization: "We literally cannot break these things. We're going to make it."

### The Nintendo Letter
**When:** January 1995
**What:** Cease-and-desist for trademark use
**Resolution:** Bob calls Nintendo legal, negotiates disclaimers
**Impact:** Crisis averted, learned to be careful with trademarks

### Maggie's Moment
**When:** AGU 2010
**Who:** Maggie Chen, Michael Rodriguez (grad student)
**What:** Student thanks Maggie for changing his career path
**Impact:** Emotional realization of legacy: "We made one person understand rocks better. Everything else is just details."
