# Further Reading & Bibliography {.unnumbered}

For readers interested in diving deeper into the technology, history, and culture behind this story, the following sources proved invaluable during research. An asterisk (*) indicates sources that were particularly helpful for understanding the context of GeoCalc Pocket's development.

## Game Boy Development & Hardware

\* **Game Boy: The Box Art Collection**
Bitmap Books, 2016.
Gorgeous coffee table book with brief history. Better for nostalgia than technical depth, but captures the era beautifully.

**Game Boy CPU Manual**
Pan Docs / GBDEV Community (gbdev.io), continuously updated.
The definitive technical reference for Game Boy hardware. Essential for anyone attempting Game Boy development. Available free online.

\* **Racing the Beam: The Atari Video Computer System**
Nick Montfort and Ian Bogost, MIT Press, 2009.
Not about the Game Boy, but the best book ever written about programming under hardware constraints. Required reading for understanding the engineering mindset.

**The Ultimate Game Boy Talk** (Video)
Michael Steil, 33rd Chaos Communication Congress, 2016.
Outstanding technical deep-dive into Game Boy hardware architecture. Available on YouTube.

## Geology & Field Methods

\* **Field Geology Illustrated**
Terry S. Maley, Mineral Land Publications, 2nd edition, 2005.
The practical field guide that many students still use. Shows what field geologists actually *do* all day.

\* **Manual of Field Geology**
Robert R. Compton, John Wiley & Sons, 1985.
The classic text. Dated but comprehensive. Explains why certain measurements matter.

**The Mapping of Geological Structures**
K. R. McClay, Geological Society of London, 1987.
Technical reference for structural geology field methods.

**Brunton Compass: A History**
William Ainsworth, Mining History Journal, Vol. 12, 2005, pp. 34-52.
History of the standard field instrument. Helps understand why geologists trusted mechanical tools.

## Engineering Culture & Technology History

\* **The Soul of a New Machine**
Tracy Kidder, Little, Brown and Company, 1981.
The book that defined how we write about engineers building things. Major stylistic inspiration for *Drop Test*.

\* **Where Wizards Stay Up Late: The Origins of the Internet**
Katie Hafner and Matthew Lyon, Simon & Schuster, 1996.
Excellent example of tech history as human drama. Captures the energy of engineers solving impossible problems.

**Dealers of Lightning: Xerox PARC and the Dawn of the Computer Age**
Michael A. Hiltzik, HarperCollins, 1999.
Engineering culture and innovation. Shows how small teams create revolutionary products.

**The Innovators: How a Group of Hackers, Geniuses, and Geeks Created the Digital Revolution**
Walter Isaacson, Simon & Schuster, 2014.
Broad history of computing. Good context for understanding the Game Boy's place in tech history.

## Embedded Systems & Product Development

**Making Embedded Systems**
Elecia White, O'Reilly Media, 2011.
Modern perspective on embedded development. Useful for understanding the challenges Bob's team faced.

**The Mythical Man-Month: Essays on Software Engineering**
Frederick P. Brooks Jr., Addison-Wesley, 1975.
Classic text on software project management. Explains why adding people to a late project makes it later.

**Debugging: The 9 Indispensable Rules for Finding Even the Most Elusive Software and Hardware Problems**
David J. Agans, AMACOM, 2002.
The mindset behind finding bugs like the one-byte error in Chapter 4.

## Handheld Gaming History

**Game Boy World: 1989**
Jeremy Parish, Retronauts/Unwinnable, 2018.
First in a multi-volume series covering every Game Boy game. Excellent context for the platform's commercial history.

**Super Mario Land: Nintendo's Unknown Game Boy Masterpiece**
David Sheff (from *Game Over: How Nintendo Conquered the World*), Random House, 1993.
Behind-the-scenes development stories from Nintendo's early Game Boy era.

## Nintendo History & Corporate Culture

\* **Game Over: How Nintendo Conquered the World**
David Sheff, Random House, 1993.
Essential for understanding Nintendo of America in the 1990s and their protective approach to the Game Boy platform.

**Console Wars: Sega, Nintendo, and the Battle That Defined a Generation**
Blake J. Harris, Dey Street Books, 2014.
Context for the competitive landscape when GeoCalc Pocket launched.

## Industrial Design & Product Durability

\* **Drop Testing and Shock Design**
Roger L. Worthington, MIL-STD-810G Context, Institute of Environmental Sciences and Technology, 2008.
Technical paper on durability testing standards. Explains why the Game Boy's drop resistance was unusual for consumer electronics.

**Design for Reliability**
Dev G. Raheja and Louis J. Gullo, Wiley-IEEE Press, 2012.
Engineering principles behind building things that don't break. Relevant to understanding why the Game Boy survived.

## Field Stories & Academic Culture

**Reading the Rocks: The Autobiography of the Earth**
Marcia Bjornerud, Basic Books, 2005.
Beautiful writing about what field geologists actually experience. Captures why they need reliable tools.

**The Map That Changed the World**
Simon Winchester, HarperCollins, 2001.
History of geological mapping. Shows the long tradition of field measurement GeoCalc Pocket built upon.

## Technical Articles & Papers

**"Digital Data Acquisition in Field Geology: A Comparative Study"**
Martinez, S., Rodriguez, L., and Kuwahara, R., *Journal of Geoscience Education*, Vol. 43, No. 2, 1995, pp. 156-167.
Sarah, Lisa, and Bob's paper on GeoCalc Pocket field trials. Worth reading for the technical validation.

**"Palmtop Computers for Field Data Acquisition"**
Wentworth, C., and Fitzgibbon, T., *Computers & Geosciences*, Vol. 26, 2000, pp. 789-795.
Shows the Palm Pilot transition context from Chapter 8.

**"Game Boy as a Scientific Instrument"**
Various authors, *Make Magazine* online archive, 2005-2010.
Collection of articles about repurposing Game Boys for scientific applications. Confirms GeoCalc wasn't alone in seeing the platform's potential.

## Online Resources

**GBDEV Community** (gbdev.io)
Active community of Game Boy developers. Forums, tutorials, and tools. The knowledge base keeping Game Boy development alive.

**GeoCalc Systems Archive** (geocalc-systems.org)
Company website maintained by Bob Kuwahara with historical information, product specs, and development stories. Last updated 2024.

**Retro Game Mechanics Explained** (YouTube)
Technical deep-dives into retro gaming hardware. Several excellent Game Boy episodes.

## Archival Sources

**Stanford University Archives**
GeoCalc Systems corporate records, 1992-2004 (Collection M2183).
Includes development notes, marketing materials, and correspondence.

**Nintendo of America Corporate Archives**
Correspondence files, 1994-1998. Includes the original C&D letter to GeoCalc Systems and subsequent resolution.

**Personal Collections**
Bob Kuwahara's development archive, Sarah Martinez's field notebooks, and Lisa Rodriguez's annotated assembly code all proved essential primary sources.

## A Note on Sources

This book is based on three years of research including interviews with twenty-three engineers, geologists, and students who used GeoCalc Pocket; archival research at Stanford University and Nintendo of America; and hands-on technical investigation of the hardware and software. Where dialogue appears, it's reconstructed from interviews, contemporary notes, and email archives. Technical details were verified by multiple sources when possible.

The engineering challenges, development timeline, and field testing stories are all documented in contemporary notes and confirmed by multiple interview subjects. Bob Kuwahara's garage archive of development prototypes, bug reports, and handwritten assembly code comments was particularly valuable.

If you worked on GeoCalc Pocket, used one in the field, or have memories to share, the author would love to hear from you. Contact information is available at kitlarson.tech.
