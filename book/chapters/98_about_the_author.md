# About the Author {.unnumbered}

**Kit Larson** is a technology journalist and former electrical engineer who specializes in stories about obsolete technology and the engineers who refuse to let it die. Their work has appeared in *Increment*, *Logic Magazine*, and *IEEE Spectrum*, and they've spent the better part of a decade tracking down the people behind forgotten innovations—from the engineers who built NASA's Voyager computers to the team that designed the last mechanical pinball machines.

Larson holds a degree in electrical engineering from the University of Washington and spent five years designing embedded systems for marine electronics before realizing they were more interested in telling stories than writing firmware. They live in Bellingham, Washington, where they maintain a collection of seventeen working Game Boys (including two original DMG-01s), a concerning number of vintage calculators, and a workshop full of oscilloscopes they insist they'll use someday.

The research for *Drop Test* took three years and included learning to use a Brunton compass, destroying two Game Boys in durability testing, and an ill-advised attempt to complete an undergraduate geology field course at age forty-two. Larson maintains that the field course was "absolutely essential for understanding the user experience" and not at all an excuse to spend six weeks in the Utah desert.

When not writing about technology, Larson can be found rock climbing (poorly), repairing vintage electronics (competently), or explaining to patient friends why the Nintendo DS was actually a remarkable piece of engineering even if they never owned one.

This is their first book.
