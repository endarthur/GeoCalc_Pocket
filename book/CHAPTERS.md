## Chapter Summaries

**Preface:** Introduction, why this story matters, methodology

**Chapter 1 - The Garage Years:**
The founding of GeoStat Instruments (later GeoStructure Systems) in 1987. Three Colorado School of Mines graduates start a field instrumentation company. Early products fail. FieldLogger II nearly bankrupts them. By late 1991: 4 months of runway left.

**Chapter 2 - The Revelation:**
December 26, 1991. Bob Kuwahara's nephew Danny drops a Game Boy down the stairs. It survives. Bob realizes: Game Boys are better field hardware than anything GeoStructure can build. He pitches the idea to Maggie and James on a napkin at a climbing gym. They give him 8 weeks to prove it.

**Chapter 3 - Eight Weeks:**
Bob, James, and Mark lock themselves in Bob's basement. Learn Z80 assembly. Fight with tile budgets, VRAM timing, fixed-point math. Week 4: first point plots correctly. Week 6: Bob's 4 AM crisis (throws Game Boy at wall, it survives). Week 8: working prototype. Maggie approves. They have 6 months to ship.

**Chapter 4 - Development Hell:**
Team expands (Sarah, Lisa, Kevin join). The tile crisis revisited. Shadow buffer breakthrough. Bug that ate September (one-byte error, 3 days to find). Manufacturing nightmare (40% of cartridges corrupted, team hand-programs 127 units over 3 days). February 1994: Launch.

**Chapter 5 - Field Testing:**
Sarah's Morrison Formation test. Rain test (it survives). Glove test (redesign needed). Sunlight problem (bolder grid lines). Drop test (6 feet, concrete, no problem). January 1995: Nintendo cease-and-desist letter. Bob calls Nintendo legal, negotiates disclaimers. Crisis averted. GeoPro GB competitor launches (fails by 1999).

**Chapter 6 - The Thesis Save:**
April 1996. Jennifer Wu, Arizona PhD student, laptop falls in creek. Three years of data lost. Backup floppy also ruined. Then she remembers: all 87 measurements are on her GeoCalc cartridge. Uploads via link cable. Thesis saved. The email. The impact. The legend. Twenty years later: reunion with Bob.

**Chapter 7 - Evolution:**
1996: GeoCalc DX with link cable (killer feature). 1998: GeoCalc Color with timestamps and color-coding. 1999: Game Boy Printer support (print at outcrop). 2001: GeoCalc Advance (bigger screen, faster). 2003: GeoCalc Advance SP (ultimate field tool - frontlit, rechargeable, clamshell). The DS decision (don't build it). Numbers: 74,580 Nintendo units sold, $26.2M revenue.

**Chapter 8 - Platform Transitions:**
1998: Palm Pilot realization. GeoCalc Palm launches (eventually outsells Game Boy). Identity crisis: Game Boy company or software company? 2004: Trimble acquisition ($23M). 2007-2010: Smartphone era (iOS, Android). Stubborn holdouts (Dr. Carlos Hernandez keeps his SP). Support commitment through 2015. Last cartridge: December 2010. What smartphones couldn't replace: the weirdness.

**Chapter 9 - The Passion Project:**
March 2011: Kevin sees Nintendo 3DS announcement (stereoscopic 3D). Idea: aerial photo interpretation with depth perception. May 2011: pitches to Maggie (20% time, $15K budget, no profit expectations). 18 months of development. December 2012: AeroGeo 3D releases (100 units, $129). Sells out in 3 months. Revenue: $12,900. Cost: $15,000. Loss: $2,100. Kevin: "Worth every penny. Zero regrets." Lessons about passion projects and keeping companies weird.

**Chapter 10 - Legacy:**
AGU 2010: Michael Rodriguez's moment ("you changed my life"). Impact metrics (340+ universities, 180+ papers, 50+ PhDs). The stories (mine collapse, Antarctica unit, drop test champions, 2.3M printouts). Educational impact (Dr. Morrison's reflection). Durability legend (60-70% still working). Cultural impact (Wired, Smithsonian, business schools). Where they are now (2013). What they built (not just software). Real legacy: the people.

**Epilogue:**
December 26, 2023 (32 years later). Bob, age 65, retired. His desk: cracked Game Boy, the napkin, Serial #001, Jennifer's dissertation, the photo. Blog post: What happened to the people (Danny is a geologist, Jennifer is a professor, Michael got his PhD, etc.). What happened to the technology (still exists on smartphones). What I learned. What I'd do differently. What happens next. To Danny: thanks for dropping that Game Boy. Final thought: pay attention, see possibilities, build weird things. The rocks remain. The measurements continue. That's enough.

**Appendix A - Technical Specifications:**
Game Boy hardware specs. GeoCalc memory map. Data structures. Projection mathematics (algorithm, code). Performance metrics. Product specs (all versions). Link cable protocol. Game Boy Printer details. File formats. Manufacturing specifications.
